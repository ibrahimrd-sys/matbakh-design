# Two hand-drawn maps, translated into Matbakh pages

**Written 6 September 2026.** Beef Enchilada Skillet and Chicken Skewers with
Thai Curry, drawn by hand on 5–6 September and translated here into the page
grammar — station by station, with the carrier named for every element: **icon,
photograph, digit or word.**

**What this is for.** `storyboard-companion.md` §7 carries two worked
storyboards, molokhia and bolognese, and says plainly that two is a sample of
two. These are the third and fourth, and unlike the first two they were **drawn
before they were mapped**, so they test the grammar against a hand that was not
following it.

**What this is not.** No decision. Every lexicon and schema observation is
logged at the foot for the ≥3 rule; nothing here meets a threshold.

---

## How to read the tables

Columns follow `storyboard-companion.md` §7, with the carrier split made
explicit.

| Column | Carrier |
|---|---|
| **Tiles** | the **icon** — one activity glyph per tile, `key` + qualifier |
| **Timer** | **digits** — attached to an action, never its own page |
| **Photo** | a **photograph**, only where a state must be judged |
| **Prose** | the only **authored words** on that page |

Amounts are digits and never appear here: they are a live layer over the
ingredient reference (`§5.4`), and they are the one thing the author does not
type twice.

### Four decisions inside that table, and why

**The crema moved from the board to the serve page.** Your sheet has it as ⑥
under BOARD. Stirring sour cream into lime juice is **bench**, not board, so on
§4.1 it cannot share the knife page — and giving it a bench page of its own for
a five-second job is exactly the over-atomisation §4.1 calls an editorial
signal. It is consumed at serve and costs nothing to make there, so it is
authored there. **Prep that takes seconds and is used at the end belongs at the
end**; a paper mise en place needs it up front, a paged reader does not.

**Cilantro and limes stay on the board even though they are also garnish.**
That is canon's own worked example — *"all knife prep — onion, garlic, tomato,
coriander — is one page with four tiles"* — and they return on page 8 as
`carried`, so the basket counts them once.

**Fold and cook are one page, not two.** *Cook 8–10 min* is a timer on the fold,
and waiting is never a page.

**Page 7 is `stove · covered`, not an oven page.** Which raises the equipment
question your sheet already implies: **you wrote *ovenproof skillet* and nothing
in the recipe ever enters the oven.** Under KS-05 that is a required-equipment
entry with no visible justification, and it is the first live test of KS-07's
`optional` flag. Either a browning step is missing, or the pan need only be a
skillet.

### Your split, and what it costs

**The board page is right, and it is a station correction rather than a layout
one.** Cutting chicken, chopping cilantro, mincing garlic, grating ginger is
board. Pouring coconut milk and fish sauce into a blender jug is **bench**. Your
"BOARD" header was covering two stations; splitting it makes the page honest and
does not change the page count.

**But page 2's `add` tile carries eight items**, and bolognese's fattest tile
carried three. On a page that may not scroll, at 393×852, that is the one
untested thing in this document.

**A cheap fix that is worth its own decision: render a `carried` item without
its amount.** The quantity was established on the page where the ingredient was
prepped; repeating it on page 2 tells the cook nothing and costs a line each.
Three of those eight items are carried, so the tile drops to five amounts and
three bare names. This is a rendering rule, not a schema change.

## Where the carriers actually land — the answer in one place

**Icons — every tile, always.** Fourteen pages across the two recipes, thirty
tiles, thirty glyphs. This is the layer that never varies, and it is why the
glyph set is owed under every option: at 17 px in the arc, nothing else survives
(`§5.5`).

**Photographs — four in two recipes.** Three doneness states in the enchilada
(the browned beef, the thickened sauce, the melted cheese), one in the skewers
(the char). Plus two board-as-orientation shots and one tight cut reference from
the Cut Library, which is a **library reference, not a per-recipe shot** —
`dice`, `mince`, `chop` and the wedge are all head-set cuts.

> **Against §16.6's open estimate of 4–6 per recipe, these two measure 3 and 1.**
> Two maps is not a measurement, but both sit at or below the bottom of the
> estimated range, and that figure is the multiplier on the largest content line
> after the build.

**Digits — every quantity, every timer, every heat level.** Never drawn, never
authored twice, and the only thing on the page that changes when the cook moves
the serving preset.

**Words — nine authored strings across two complete recipes**, plus a `why` for
each:

| | Enchilada | Skewers |
|---|---|---|
| Doneness cues | 3 | 1 |
| Notes | 1 (`stay_here`) | 2 (`make_ahead`, `stay_here`) |
| Step qualifiers | 3 | 3 |
| Bespoke verbs | 0 | 1 |

Everything a reader sees beyond those — the verbs in five locales, the station
headers, the unit symbols, the page counter, the whole prose view — is generated
from the object. **That is the wordless claim stated as a number: a complete
recipe is about five authored strings.**

## Logged for the ≥3 rule — no thresholds met

**Two glyphs would draw the wrong thing, both live in these recipes.**
`toast` resolves to the glyph `toast` — a slice of bread — on a page that toasts
ground spices, and `garnish` resolves to `leaf` on a page garnishing with
cilantro *and* lime wedges *and* chili flakes. Both are on C-05's
ingredient-drawing list; this is the first time they appear in a drawn recipe.

**`season` and `to_taste` co-occur again.** Page 3 seasons, page 8 finishes to
taste, and they share `salt.svg`. Different pages, so it does not bite — the
same luck molokhia had. **Second recipe in a row where the collision is
survived by accident rather than by design.**

**`brush` / `baste` is genuinely ambiguous here rather than colliding.**
Brushing reserved marinade onto skewers *during* grilling is `baste` by
station and `brush` by gesture. The shared glyph means the reader cannot tell —
but neither can the author, which is a different and more interesting problem
than a collision.

**`char` appears as a doneness state, not an act.** Nobody chars; they grill
until charred. It currently holds one of the 81 entries and a glyph.

**Two cuts the vocabulary does not name:** tortillas *into squares* and chicken
*into strips*. Both are carried as `slice` + qualifier today. PM-12 input.

**One bespoke verb:** *"Set aside a third"*. The escape hatch working as
designed — and it exists because **the schema cannot partition an in-recipe
intermediate by fraction.** `@marinade` can be referenced with a measured
amount, as molokhia references `@stock` at 1200 ml, but a blended marinade has
no yield a cook ever measures. A ratio is also **scale-invariant** where a
millilitre figure is not: at eight servings, a third is still a third. That
argues for `fraction_of` on an intermediate reference rather than for a
workaround.

**One page shape the reader has no model for.** Page 5 of the skewers requires
turning and brushing *throughout* a five-minute timer. Tile state is
done/not-done (`§4.3`) and the timer band reports what the stove is doing
without you (`§4.6`); neither expresses *keep doing this while this runs*.
`do_not_stir` is the exact inverse and was kept as its own activity because
inverting it misleads on a 44 px tile. This is the positive case, and it is
currently carried by a `stay_here` note and a qualifier — which works, and is
worth knowing is a workaround.

**Data hygiene, both maps:** unit abbreviations drift (*t*, *ts*, *T*, *Tb*),
and the enchilada's quantity column says **crème fraîche** where the map says
**sour cream**. Both are caught by authoring in `recipe-editor.html` against the
closed unit set and the ingredient reference, and neither is caught on paper.
